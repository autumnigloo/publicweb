How this works

A Gamble puts the top three cards of the Fate deck in a sequence, then lets you replace any number of them with cards from your hand. So a Gamble succeeds if any three cards out of (the three you draw + your whole hand) make the rank.

Tell the app which cards you're holding and which are in the discard pile; everything else is still in the deck. It then checks every possible draw from the remaining deck — no simulation, these are exact odds.

Moving cards — the Hand / Discard toggle picks where a tap sends a card. Tap any card to send it to that pile; tap a card already sitting there to put it back in the deck. Set the toggle to match what you're doing — Hand while drawing in the Refresh step, Discard while resolving a Gamble — and every card is one tap. Each pile's header shows where its cards will go.

Cards drawn is three by default. Set it higher for effects that deal a longer sequence, such as The Moon. A longer sequence cuts both ways: Pair, Army, Successors and Rulers only need the sequence to contain something, so more cards make them easier — but Color needs every card in the sequence to share a suit, so more cards make it harder (beyond five it becomes impossible, as no suit has that many cards even with the Jester).

Ranks

  • Pair — two figures of the same kind
  • Army — three Knights
  • Successors — a Prince and a Princess
  • Rulers — a King and a Queen
  • Color — all cards in the same suit

The 14-card Fate deck
1× Hanged Man (Black) · 1× Jester (White) · 1× King (Violet) · 1× Queen (Yellow) · 2× Princess (Yellow, Red) · 2× Prince (Red, Violet) · 6× Knight (2 Red, 2 Violet, 2 Yellow).

The Jester counts as any figure in any suit. The Hanged Man is its own fourth suit, so it forms no figure ranks and can never complete a Color.

Discards reshuffle into the deck after a Pass, or when the deck runs out mid-draw — the app handles that automatically when fewer than three cards remain.
